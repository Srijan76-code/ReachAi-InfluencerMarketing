---
title: Writing a good brief
description: Small changes to your brief make a big difference to who you get.
---

ReachAI ranks creators by how well their content matches **your product and your buyer**, not by follower count. The clearer the brief, the better the matches.

## Describe the outcome, not the category

<Callout type="info" title="Example">
  ❌ "An edtech app"

  ✅ "A 12-week data science bootcamp that helps fresh graduates get their first ML job, with a refund if they don't."
</Callout>

ReachAI turns the outcome into specific topics and search terms, so a concrete outcome finds creators who talk about that exact problem.

## Be specific about the buyer

Write who buys and what stage they're at: "final-year CS students applying for internships" is much better than "students". The **stage** matters: beginners and experts follow different creators.

## Pick the right goal and creator type

| If you want… | Choose |
|---|---|
| Lots of people to hear about you | **Brand awareness**; peer/relatable creators work well |
| Signups or trials | **Leads / signups** |
| Purchases, especially for higher-priced products | **Sales / conversions** with **expert** or **mentor** creators |

## Set a realistic creator size

Smaller creators (for example 10k–100k subscribers) are often cheaper and closer to their audience. If you set a very high minimum, you'll see fewer creators.

## Price range changes the bar

For **high-priced** products ReachAI accepts creators with smaller view counts, because fewer, better-fit viewers can still pay off. For **low-priced** products it asks for more reach.
